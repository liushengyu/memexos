# Luhmann's slip-box, explained

An article on how [[Niklas Luhmann]] built and used his [[Zettelkasten]], and how the method is taught today.

## Key points

- Luhmann (1927–1998), a German sociologist, credited his productivity largely to his slip-box.
- Over more than four decades he filled it with **about 90,000 index cards**, one idea per card, in his own words.
- Cards were filed next to the card they continued; fixed numbers recorded the position (21/3, then 21/4; a side
  thought on 21/3 became 21/3a), so any card could be referenced from anywhere.
- Links between cards and a short keyword index of entry cards let him follow chains of related ideas, often to
  connections he had not planned.
- In the 1981 essay *Kommunikation mit Zettelkästen* he described the box as a conversation partner.
- The box went to Bielefeld University, where the Niklas Luhmann archive is digitising the cards and publishing them online.
- Sönke Ahrens' *How to Take Smart Notes* (2017) popularised the method: short notes while reading, then
  [[Permanent note]]s that stand alone and link to existing notes.
