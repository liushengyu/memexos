# Zettelkasten

A Zettelkasten (slip-box) is a collection of short notes, one idea each, organised by links between notes rather than
by topic; the best-known example is [[Niklas Luhmann]]'s ([[Luhmann's slip-box, explained]]).

## How Luhmann's worked

- About 90,000 index cards, one idea per card, in his own words.
- A new card was filed next to the card it continued, with a fixed number recording that position (21/3, 21/4; a side
  thought on 21/3 became 21/3a), so a card could be referenced from anywhere.
- Cards referenced related cards by number, and a short keyword index pointed to a few entry cards per subject.
- Following references led through chains of ideas, often to connections he had not planned; he described the box as a
  conversation partner (1981).

## The method today

Popularised by Sönke Ahrens' *How to Take Smart Notes* (2017): short notes while reading, then [[Permanent note]]s that
stand alone and link to existing notes.
