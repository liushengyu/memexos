# Spaced repetition

Spaced repetition is a way of keeping what you learned: you review an item just before you would forget it, and each
successful review pushes the next review further into the future ([[How spaced repetition works]]).

## Why it works

It applies two findings attributed to [[Hermann Ebbinghaus]]: memory fades along the [[Forgetting curve]], fast at
first and then more slowly, and reviews spread over days hold better than the same reviews crammed together (the
spacing effect).

## Ways to schedule reviews

| Method | Year | How the next review is chosen |
|---|---|---|
| [[Leitner system]] | 1972 | by box: a right answer moves the card up a box, a wrong one sends it back to the first |
| [[SM-2]] | 1987 | per card: the interval grows by the card's ease factor after each successful review |
| FSRS (in [[Anki]] since 23.10) | 2023 | per card, from a memory model fitted to the user's own review history |

## Good practice

- One fact per card; a card asking for a long list fails for one missing item.
- Write cards in your own words, after understanding the idea.
- Review a little every day rather than a lot once a week.
