# Remembering what you read

An overview of two complementary practices for keeping what you read: [[Spaced repetition]] to recall facts, and
[[Permanent note]]s in a [[Zettelkasten]] to keep and connect ideas, and how the owner combined them.

## Why reading alone is not enough

Memory fades along the [[Forgetting curve]]: steeply within the first day, then more slowly
([[How spaced repetition works]]). Reviewing at growing intervals counters it; writing ideas down in your own words
keeps what does not need to be recalled from memory ([[Luhmann's slip-box, explained]]).

## Recall: spaced repetition

- Scheduling evolved from paper boxes ([[Leitner system]], 1972) to per-card algorithms ([[SM-2]], 1987) and to
  schedulers fitted to a user's own history (FSRS, in [[Anki]] since 23.10).
- Cards work best small, in your own words, and reviewed a little every day.

## Understanding: notes that link

- [[Niklas Luhmann]] kept about 90,000 cards, one idea each, linked by fixed numbers; following links surfaced
  connections he had not planned.
- Permanent notes are the modern form: stand-alone, in your own words, linked to existing notes.

## The owner's routine

Decided on 2026-01-12 after paper Leitner boxes failed within two weeks ([[Setting up a reading and review routine]]):
one or two permanent notes per article; at most three flashcards per article, one fact each; Anki with FSRS; fifteen
minutes every morning. Whether fifteen minutes stays enough for a larger deck is still open.
