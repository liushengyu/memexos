memex: the command line of a knowledge base ($vault). Any agent uses it to search the knowledge base and to put things into it.

When to use it
  When a question touches what this knowledge base covers ($domains), or what its owner has worked on, decided or learned,
  search first and answer from it. If it has nothing, say so, answer from your own knowledge, and say which part is not from it.

Search
  memex search "<question>" --kw "<2–5 keywords>"
      Finds pages and returns their kind, classification, which keywords each contains, and an excerpt. Pick the
      keywords yourself (names, terms) in the language the pages are written in ($language): that works better than
      the question alone. Nothing found: try other keywords or other wording.
  memex get <page name or path>
      One page in full (a name as search shows it, a [[link]] or a path). Pages live in wiki/: topics (overviews, start
      there), concepts, entities (people, tools), sources (one summary per source).
  memex catalog list --domain <domain> --evidence <evidence>
      Filter sources by domain and evidence strength (memex catalog stats lists the domains).
  memex feedback <page> --kind incorrect|outdated|missing --why "<reason>"
      Report a page that is wrong, outdated or missing something you needed (add --evidence "<source>" when you have one).
      Reports are checked against the original sources before anything changes.

Add things (only into raw/; compiling and review happen automatically)
  memex add-url <link>     articles, tweets, podcasts, videos
  memex add-video <link>   videos (subtitles when available, otherwise local transcription)
  A conclusion, decision, pitfall or measurement worth keeping came out of your conversation: ask the person whether to
  keep it, and if they agree write a session snapshot (format: memex help snapshot).

Rules for answering
  1. Cite: say which page you used (wiki/…). Key facts can be traced through the page's Sources section to raw/.
  2. Copy numbers, units, names and quotes exactly as the page has them.
  3. Do not overstate: a page saying "target about 95%" does not mean "measured 95%"; "most" does not mean "all".
  4. Word claims by the source's evidence strength (page frontmatter `evidence`): first-hand tests, official or original
     authors and academic research can support facts (say who, under what conditions); a third-party report becomes
     "according to …"; a personal opinion becomes "the author thinks"; promotional material is not evidence.
     Keep "⚠️ unverified" and "(compiler's inference)" markers when you repeat such text; date short-lived facts.
  5. Do not edit wiki/ or index.md directly (they go through compiling and review); use memex feedback to report problems.

More
  memex <command> --help   options and examples (search, get, catalog, add-url, add-video, feedback, usage)
  memex help snapshot      how to write a session snapshot
  memex help admin         maintenance commands (compiling, review, collection, status), usually run by the scheduler
